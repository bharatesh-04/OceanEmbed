Latent space

Each ocean cell is encoded into a 32-dimensional vector, then projected to two dimensions. Cells separate by water mass without ever being told which basin they came from — that structure is what the decoder reads.

32-d embedding, projected

Skill is scored against held-out profiles: fifteen levels, 900 random ocean cells for the day, plus simulated gridded-ARGO matchups carrying instrument noise. Everything recomputes when you change the day, the inputs or the architecture.

Where the error lives

Absolute error at the selected level. It clusters on sharp gradients — the Somali and Oman upwelling fronts, eddy rims, and the freshwater-capped northern Bay of Bengal — which matches what basin-scale reconstructions report elsewhere.

Dropping sea level anomaly can improve mixed-layer scores while degrading the thermocline. That is not a bug. Altimetry carries the thermocline-displacement signal the decoder needs at depth, but its mesoscale noise leaks into near-surface levels where SST already pins the answer.

From five satellite streams to a three-dimensional field

Harmonise

Every product is conservatively remapped to a common 0.25° daily grid, then gap-filled and land-masked.

Encode

A nine-channel surface stack over a 32×32 patch is compressed to a 32-d latent vector per cell.

Decode

Latent vector plus position and day-of-year maps to temperature at fifteen standard depths.

Score

RMSE, bias and correlation per level against GLORYS, then independently against gridded ARGO.

The gradient penalty matters more than it sounds. A plain per-level loss gives profiles that are individually close but vertically ragged, so the diagnosed thermocline depth wanders from level to level. Penalising the vertical derivative keeps the reconstructed thermocline coherent, and the thermocline is what downstream heat-content, marine-heatwave and fisheries products actually read off the profile.

What this build is

A working front end over a physically parameterised ocean. Mixed-layer depth responds to wind stress and salinity stratification, thermocline depth responds to sea level anomaly and eddies, and the northern Bay of Bengal grows a barrier layer with a temperature inversion in winter. It exists so the interface, the diagnostics and the validation harness can be built and reviewed before the real archive lands. Replace surfaceState() with a NetCDF reader and reconParams() with a call to your trained checkpoint; nothing else on the page has to change.

Reference points from the literature

  • A North Atlantic reconstruction from SST, SSH/SLA, winds and SST gradient reports roughly 0.49 °C RMSE overall, with error concentrated in the thermocline and along the Gulf Stream, and SST the dominant predictor at every depth. Taylor & Francis, 2026
  • TS-Cast, over the northwestern Pacific, keeps temperature RMSE under about 1 °C in the upper 500 m, peaking at the main thermocline. Ocean Science, 2026
  • A global ConvLSTM product finds skill falling fastest between 100 and 500 m, where mixed-layer and thermocline processes are least visible from the surface. Remote Sensing, 2022
  • A dual-path CNN places vertical errors mostly in the upper ocean, in the 0.2 to 1.0 °C range. JMSE, 2023
